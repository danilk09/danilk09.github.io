The Honolulu Annual Code Challenge (HACC) is a coding competition where amateurs up to professional programmers can develop solutions for real problems within the community of Hawaii. The competition I participated in lasted one month. The project my team selected was to develop a web application that standardized how projects and their respective reports were viewed or edited by Enterprise Technology Services (ETS) empoloyees, Independent Verification and Validation (IV&V) vendors, and the public.

Within the first week, our team decided on the tech stack, made flowcharts, and produced mock web pages in Figma. These steps aided in facilitating an efficient development process for the following weeks. Afterwards, we started coding both the back-end and front-end. Two team members focused on the back-end using Firebase to handle user authentication, database, and deployment. Firebase aided in streamlining the account creation process and session management to integrate it within the React application. The rest of the team worked on the front-end, which included myself. While waiting for the ability to pull actual data from the database, we developed the pages planned in Figma along with their components. Once the database was set up, each page and their compenents were converted to use data from the database. In the days preceding the project submission, our team continually tested and fixed bugs in the webpage.
